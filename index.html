How this works, and what it can't do

The text is split into passages of about 100 words. Each passage gets 26 measurements of its style, and a statistical model turns them into a score. Each sentence then gets its passage's score, adjusted by anything specific to that sentence.

  • Trained, not guessed: the model was fitted with logistic regression on 443 texts: 342 human texts written before 2021 (Wikipedia articles and discussion pages, Hacker News comments, Stack Exchange answers and Mastodon posts, in English, German and Dutch) and 101 AI texts in many genres, from essays and emails to stories, reviews and forum answers.
  • Measured accuracy: in 5-fold cross-validation, where every text was scored by a model that had never seen it, Textprint marked at least half of the text as AI-style for 81% of the AI texts (English 85%, German 70%, Dutch 80%) and for 3.5% of the human texts. The previous version caught 52% and wrongly flagged 4.1%.
  • Style: how evenly sentence lengths run, word length, abstract nouns, how varied the vocabulary is, trailing "-ing" clauses (", ensuring…"), how polished the punctuation is, and how evenly sized the paragraphs are.
  • Specifics: names, numbers, technical terms, asides in parentheses, quotes and questions lean human. Generic text without them leans AI.
  • Wording: words and stock phrases that language models overuse ("enhance", "crucial", "delve", "a testament to", "it's important to note"), in all three languages.
  • Formatting: markdown bold and headings, bullets with bold lead-ins, emoji bullets.
  • Hidden characters: zero-width characters, unusual spaces, direction controls, Unicode tag characters and variation-selector runs (which can carry a hidden message, decoded here), and look-alike letters from other alphabets.

What the numbers don't tell you. The AI test texts come from one model family (Claude). Text from other AI models, AI text that a person edited, and kinds of writing that weren't in the test can score differently, so expect lower accuracy in real use. No detector is 100% accurate, this one included. Claude and GPT don't put a detectable watermark in their text, so no tool can prove who wrote something. Hidden characters prove the text passed through some tool or editor, not that an AI wrote it. Use the score as a reason to look closer, never as proof against a person.

It doesn't name the model (Claude, GPT, Gemini). Their styles overlap too much to tell apart reliably.
